    Gold and US interest rates

    Why gold falls when America pays more to borrow

    Gold earns nothing. When the real return on a US Treasury bond rises, holding gold costs you that return, so money leaves gold. The copper line is the 10-year real yield; when it climbs, the black line tends to fall. The grey dashed line is the ordinary 10-year yield before inflation.

    How to read it. Left axis is gold in dollars, right axis is yield in %. The two yields differ by expected inflation: the grey line is what a US bond pays, the copper line is what it pays after inflation, and gold answers to the copper line because a gold holder gives up the real return, not the nominal one. When the copper line climbs, gold tends to fall, as it has since March. The turn to watch for is the copper line rolling over, not gold "looking cheap".

    What China pays

    Is Shanghai paying more for gold than London?

    China is the largest physical buyer. The Shanghai Gold Exchange sets a benchmark twice a day in yuan per gram. When Shanghai pays more than the London price converted to yuan, Chinese demand is pulling metal east and supports the world price. A discount means Chinese buyers have stepped back.
